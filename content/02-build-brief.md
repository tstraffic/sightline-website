## 1. Who the site is written for
Four markets, weighted evenly. That has one direct consequence for the build: the homepage stays neutral and the sector pages carry the
targeting. A civil contractor and an architect want opposite things from a first paragraph — one is worried about a programme slipping, the other
about a DA stalling. Speaking to both at once speaks to neither.

Writing rules that follow from this
Name the buyer’s problem, not our service. “Council asked for a traffic report” lands. “We provide Traffic Impact Statements” does not.
Don’t rewrite service pages per sector. The service pages stay as written and serve all four markets. Only the sector pages change voice.
Every sector page ends in a route, not a pitch — three to five links to the pages that buyer needs.

#### Section 3 — Why Sightline
Anyone can draw a plan. Fewer can defend it.
A TGS is a certified engineering document. So is a barrier cross section, a swept path model, and a capacity analysis. We treat them that way.
Engineered, not templated. Every scheme is designed to the site — geometry, sightlines, volumes, staging — not adapted from the last job
that looked similar.
Checked before it is issued. Barrier deflection zones against the manufacturer’s specification and the road authority’s approved list,
whichever is stricter. Swept paths reconfirmed at every stage.
Held together across stages. Multi-stage projects get one coherent framework — staging, barriers, swept paths and network impact
designed together.
Getting it right at design stage costs a fraction of fixing it after construction, or after a rejection.

4.1 Work Zone Engineering

#### Practice landing page — intro copy
A work zone is an engineering problem before it is a paperwork problem.
Lane widths, barrier deflection, sightlines, swept paths, staged handovers, network impact — get any of them wrong and the consequence is a
rejection, a delay, or someone hurt. Sightline designs the full documentation set for works on live road networks: Traffic Guidance Schemes,
staging sets, Traffic Management Plans, barrier design statements, and independent site audits.
Prepared to TCAWS, AS 1742.3 and the Austroads Guide to Temporary Traffic Management — and prepared by engineers, which is the part
that shows up when the job stops matching the plan.

4.3 Development & Parking Engineering

#### Practice landing page — intro copy
A DA does not fail on architecture. It fails on a dimension.
Bay widths, ramp transitions, sight triangles, a design vehicle that cannot physically reach the loading dock — parking and access non-
compliance is one of the most common and most preventable reasons council knocks a development application back.
Sightline provides the technical evidence: TIS and TIA reporting, carpark demand assessments, AS 2890 compliance certification, driveway
design for constrained sites, sightline assessments and vehicle access reviews.
