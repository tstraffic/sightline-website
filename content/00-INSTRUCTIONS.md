## Important: this email overrides conflicting instructions in the attached documents

The following decisions are now confirmed.

### Approved brand

The website must use the approved **Oxide Mark / Technical Editorial** identity, not Forest Green and Brass.

The approved palette is:

* Aubergine: `#3E2632`
* Oxide: `#A34652`
* Carbon: `#24272A`
* Survey Grey: `#657278`
* Mineral Dust: `#E5E1DE`
* Drawing Paper: `#F8F6F2`

Typography:

* Hanken Grotesk for headings, body text, navigation and buttons
* IBM Plex Mono for project numbers, drawing references, revisions, status labels and other technical details

The visual character should be:

* Independent
* Technically authoritative
* Precise
* Elegant
* Approachable
* Predominantly light, with controlled Aubergine sections
* Inspired by engineering documentation rather than generic corporate design

Please use the approved capability-statement cover as the closest visual reference for the website's technical road-diagram treatment, logo use, typography and restrained Oxide accents.

"Designed to standard." remains the tagline.

"Technical authority, clearly expressed." is the broader brand idea and does not need to appear as the main headline on every page.

### Site size and structure

Please scope the website as approximately **39 public URLs**, comprising:

* Homepage
* Three Engineering Services practice landing pages
* Approvals & Delivery Support landing page
* Sectors landing page
* 25 individual service pages
* Five sector pages
* Projects
* About
* Contact

This does not mean 39 separate layouts. The website should use reusable templates and components.

Recommended page templates:

1. Homepage
2. Practice or section landing page
3. Individual service page
4. Sector page
5. Projects index
6. Case-study page
7. About
8. Contact

### Main navigation

* Home
* Engineering Services
* Approvals
* Sectors
* Projects
* About
* Contact

Engineering Services contains:

* Work Zone Engineering
* Transport & Network Engineering
* Development & Parking Engineering

Approvals & Delivery Support remains a top-level section because it contains important search pages such as ROL and Council Permits.

### Four equal service pillars

The homepage should present all four pillars with equal importance:

1. Work Zone Engineering
2. Transport & Network Engineering
3. Development & Parking Engineering
4. Approvals & Delivery Support

The earlier brief presented three engineering cards followed by a separate approvals band. Please revise this so Approvals is not treated as an afterthought.

### Markets and sector pages

There are four strategic audience groups but five public sector pages:

* Civil Contractors
* Developers & Architects
* Government & Council
* Utilities
* Events

Utilities and Events remain separate public pages because they have different problems, search intent and service pathways.

The homepage remains neutral. Sector-specific language belongs on the sector pages. The existing copy strategy requires sector pages to name each buyer's actual problem and then route them to the relevant services, rather than repeating generic service descriptions.

### Core writing rule

**Name the buyer's problem, not the service.**

For example:

* "Council asked for a traffic report" is stronger than "We provide Traffic Impact Statements."
* "Your programme doesn't wait for a redrawn plan" is stronger than "We provide staging services."

Service pages should still explain the technical service in detail, but their openings should lead with the practical problem, risk or decision the visitor is facing.

### Primary calls to action

Please standardise the main actions across the site.

**Primary CTA:**
Request a fee proposal

**Secondary CTA:**
View our services

**Contact/form line:**
Send us the drawings. We'll tell you what you need.

Avoid introducing several competing phrases such as "request a scope", "get started", "contact us today" and "book a consultation".

### Standalone company positioning

Sightline should be presented as a standalone traffic and transport engineering practice.

Do not lead the homepage with its relationship to T&S Traffic Control.

Practical traffic-management and delivery experience can remain as supporting evidence, but Sightline must lead with:

* Its engineers
* Its technical services
* Its standards
* Its quality-control process
* Its independent professional judgment

Signage installation is not a Sightline service and should not appear on this website.

Do not casually shorten or remove:

* Standards references
* Process steps
* TIS versus TIA explanations

The highest-priority search pages must retain their individual URLs and substantive content:

* TGS
* TCP
* CTMP
* ROL
* Council Permits

The existing brief specifically identifies these as major search terms and says their pages should not be combined away.

### Pages requiring new copy

#### Road Safety Audits

Create a brief for an independent road-safety review service, distinct from a temporary traffic-management Site Audit.

The page should cover:

* Design-stage and pre-opening audits
* Independent review
* Road-user safety risks
* Site inspection
* Findings and risk classification
* Recommendations
* Designer responses and close-out
* Auditor qualifications, once confirmed

#### Traffic Data Collection & Analysis

Keep this separate from Parking & Traffic Surveys, but clearly divide their purpose.

Traffic Data Collection should focus on:

* Intersection and network studies
* Turning-movement counts
* Classified vehicle counts
* Queue observations
* Travel time
* Speed data
* Pedestrian and cyclist counts
* Model calibration
* Evidence for SIDRA, Aimsun, TIAs and road-network analysis

Parking & Traffic Surveys should retain its development and parking focus:

* Occupancy
* Accumulation
* Duration
* Turnover
* Parking demand
* Number-plate studies
* Evidence supporting parking assessments

#### Operational Traffic & Parking Management Plans

This is confirmed as a standalone service.

It concerns ongoing operation after a site opens, rather than temporary construction traffic management.

The page should cover:

* Internal vehicle circulation
* Staff, customer, resident and visitor parking
* Loading and delivery arrangements
* Waste collection
* Pick-up and drop-off
* Queue management
* Accessible parking
* Pedestrian movement
* Signage and line marking
* Responsibilities and monitoring
* Review and complaint procedures

Likely markets include car parks, schools, childcare centres, medical facilities, places of worship, shopping centres, residential developments and industrial sites.

#### Road Design & CAD Documentation

Retain this page in the sitemap but keep it on hold until its precise scope is confirmed.

CAD is the tool, not the actual service. The eventual page must explain the design drawings Sightline provides.

Potential inclusions to confirm with the engineering team are:

* Road and intersection layouts
* Linemarking and signage plans
* Kerb, lane and median geometry
* Access and driveway plans
* General arrangement drawings
* Longitudinal sections
* Cross-sections
* Design details
* Authority-submission drawing sets
* Concept, detailed or construction-ready documentation

Please do not draft claims until Sightline confirms which of these it genuinely delivers.

#### About

The About page must feature named engineers rather than generic company language.

It should cover:

* Names and roles
* Degrees and qualifications
* Professional registrations
* Relevant accreditations
* Areas of responsibility
* Experience
* Standards and systems
* Internal checking and approval process
* Independent practice positioning
* Practical delivery understanding

We will separately provide the confirmed credentials and details that may be published.

### Tone

Use:

* Plain
* Precise
* Confident
* Human
* Sentence case
* Short headings
* Active voice
* Specific technical language where useful

Avoid:

* Exclamation marks
* Generic "solutions" language
* "Industry-leading" without evidence
* "World-class"
* "Maximising safety"
* "Take the hassle out"
* "Approved first time" unless substantiated
* "Faster approval" without evidence
* Claims that Sightline can guarantee authority decisions

Strong existing lines worth retaining include:

* Designed to standard.
* Anyone can draw a plan. Fewer can defend it.
* A work zone is an engineering problem before it is a paperwork problem.
* One TGS is straightforward. A dozen, changing week to week, is a different problem entirely.
* Statutory parking rates don't know your site—a demand assessment does.
* Your programme doesn't wait for a redrawn plan.
* Council asked for a traffic report. We'll tell you which one.
* Fifty small jobs is not one big job.
* Send us the drawings. We'll tell you what you need.

* Page title

### Unfinished pages

Build unfinished pages in the CMS, but do not publish empty public placeholders.

They should remain:

* Draft
* Hidden from navigation
* Unindexed

until approved copy is supplied.

### Technical and visual requirements

The site should:

* Remain predominantly Drawing Paper and Mineral Dust
* Use controlled Aubergine hero and conversion sections
* Use Oxide sparingly for links, rules, active states and technical annotation
* Use genuine engineering diagram language
* Avoid random CAD overlays
* Avoid green, brass, hi-vis yellow and orange
* Avoid heavy gradients, glass effects and large rounded SaaS-style cards
* Use one clear primary CTA per visual field
* Be fully responsive
* Meet current accessibility and contrast requirements
* Optimise image and font loading
* Provide editable CMS fields rather than hard-coded page copy
